Federal Decree-Law No. 9 of 2024 amended the UAE Labour Law with effect from **31 August 2024**. The changes mainly affect how labour disputes are raised, decided and enforced. They matter to employees thinking about a claim and to HR teams managing exits and disputes.

## 1. Two years to bring a claim

Employers and employees now have **two years from the end of employment** to bring a labour claim. Before the amendment, the limit was one year.

**For HR:** keep exit records, final settlement calculations, signed acknowledgements and correspondence for at least the claim window, and ideally longer under your retention policy.

## 2. MOHRE decisions on smaller claims are enforceable

For disputes valued at **AED 50,000 or less**, MOHRE decisions now carry the force of a **writ of execution**. This means they can be enforced like a court judgment, without first going through a full court case. Larger disputes still follow the normal court process.

**For HR:** treat a MOHRE hearing on a small claim as seriously as court. Bring complete, organised evidence the first time.

## 3. A new appeal route

A party who disagrees with a MOHRE decision now goes to the **Court of First Instance**, not the Court of Appeal.

## 4. Wages during a dispute

Where wages have been stopped because of a dispute, MOHRE can instruct the employer to **continue paying wages for up to two months** while the dispute is resolved.

**For HR:** stopping pay as leverage in a dispute is risky. Take advice before suspending wages.

## 5. Higher penalties

The amendments increased fines for certain labour law violations, with published summaries describing a range of **AED 100,000 to AED 1,000,000** depending on the violation. The decree also targets fictitious employment practices.

## What HR teams should do now

1. Update exit and records-retention procedures to reflect the two-year window.
2. Make final settlements accurate and on time. Use clear breakdowns that employees can check.
3. Prepare a standard evidence pack for disputes: contract, payslips, attendance, leave records, warnings and settlement calculation.
4. Train managers that disputes now move faster and smaller MOHRE decisions are directly enforceable.
