The Wage Protection System (WPS) has been part of UAE payroll for years, but the rules changed on **1 June 2026**. Ministerial Resolution No. 340 of 2026 replaced Ministerial Resolution No. 598 of 2022 and applies to private-sector establishments licensed with the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE).

If your payroll calendar, deduction practice or onboarding process was built around the old rules, some of it now needs to change.

## The short version

* **One due date for everyone:** wages for a calendar month are due on the **first day of the following month**.
* **Higher compliance threshold:** an establishment needs to transfer **at least 85%** of total wages due by the due date (previously 80%).
* **New joiners are covered from day one:** the old 30-day exemption for new employees has gone.
* **Faster escalation:** warnings can start from the second day after the due date, and new work permits can be suspended from the fifth day.

## 1. A single wage due date

Under the new resolution, wages for a Gregorian month are due on the **1st of the next month**. Anything later counts as delayed.

Paying earlier, for example on the last working day of the month, is generally understood to be compliant. The real impact falls on employers who pay in arrears around the middle of the following month. If that is you, your payroll calendar needs to move.

**What to do:** map your current cut-off, approval and bank-file dates backwards from the 1st. Leave a buffer for bank holidays and weekends.

## 2. The 85% rule and deductions

An establishment is treated as compliant if it pays at least **85% of the total wages due** by the due date. An individual worker also counts as paid when the shortfall is due to lawful deductions under the Labour Law.

In practice, this means deductions passing through WPS should stay **within 15% of monthly wages**, even where the Labour Law itself allows more in specific cases such as approved loan recovery.

**What to do:** review salary advance and loan recovery schedules. Spread recoveries so no month's deduction pushes an employee below 85% of wages due.

## 3. The escalation timeline

Counting from the day after the due date:

| Day | What can happen |
|---|---|
| 1 | Electronic monitoring begins |
| 2 | MOHRE may issue notices and warnings |
| 5 | Issuance of new work permits may be suspended |
| 11 | Repeat violations within six months may lead to fines and reclassification to the Third Category |
| 16 | For larger employers in specified sectors, labour disputes may be registered automatically |
| 21 | More severe measures, including precautionary attachment, travel bans and referral to the Public Prosecutor |

The sectors singled out for the heavier measures include construction, transport and storage, security, cleaning and recruitment.

## 4. New joiners from day one

Previously, new employees had a 30-day grace period before WPS applied. That exemption has been removed, so **a new hire's first salary is in scope immediately**.

**What to do:** make sure bank details, labour card and WPS registration are complete before the first payroll after joining. Build this into your onboarding checklist, not just your payroll checklist.

## 5. Third-party payroll is allowed, but responsibility stays with you

Employers can delegate payroll processing to a third party, as long as MOHRE has the delegate's details and scope. The employer remains fully responsible for paying on time.

## A practical checklist

1. Move your payroll calendar so salaries reach employees by the 1st.
2. Check that every active employee, including joiners in their first month, is set up for WPS.
3. Cap WPS deductions at 15% of monthly wages unless you have confirmed otherwise.
4. Agree an escalation owner for any failed or rejected salary file, with same-day follow-up.
5. Keep evidence of every wage payment, ready to submit if asked.
6. If you use a payroll provider, confirm MOHRE has been notified and review your service levels against the new dates.
